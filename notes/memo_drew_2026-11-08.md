A quick update on the conflict–unrest paper. The draft is current on Overleaf. The abstract, introduction and discussion are still blank for you.

**Main result: escalation from unrest to conflict**
- When we model shared episodes and unrest-only episodes separately, the escalation effect of past unrest on conflict is about 0.10 on the logit scale. That holds in both regions:
  - West Africa: 0.095 (SE 0.032);
  - Africa and the Middle East: 0.105 (SE 0.015).
- Models of each outcome alone give 0.13–0.15. Shared, unobserved episodes account for about a quarter to a third of that association.
- A model with only one shared field understates escalation (0.04–0.06). It misreads unrest's own episodes as shared. The simulations reproduce this, and the extended model corrects it.

**Prediction**
- Overall, the joint model does not forecast co-occurrence better than separate models, in either region or in the simulations.
- Some areas do improve. We picked them on 2022 and checked them on 2023–24, without refitting the model:
  - Afghanistan (+0.015 per cell-week, but uncertain);
  - northern Algeria and Tunisia (+0.010, precise);
  - Nigeria and Burkina Faso.
- The model does worse in Yemen and Sudan, where the 2018–22 relationship broke down (in Sudan, after the war began in April 2023).
